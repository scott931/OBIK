ABOUT OBIK • WHO WE ARE

Improving the experience of ageing for women.

OBIK — Strengthening the support systems women need to age with dignity.

OBIK is an AgeTech and social impact enterprise developing integrated technology and support systems that strengthen the wellbeing, independence and quality of life of older women.

We recognize that ageing brings changing needs, and that access to financial resources, healthcare and reliable practical support can make a significant difference in a woman's quality of life.

Yet these resources are not equally accessible.

Across African communities, many older women navigate financial insecurity, healthcare barriers and limited support networks. Families may be separated by migration and work, while some women have little or no dependable family support.

OBIK works to address these gaps through interconnected programmes, institutional partnerships and community-based solutions.

Our work is centered on strengthening access, improving coordination and building support systems that enable women to maintain their dignity and independence as they age.

Our Focus

Strengthening access, improving coordination, and building dignified support systems.

01 • Direction

Our Vision

An Africa where every woman can age with dignity, independence and access to the support she needs to live well.

02 • Action

Our Mission

To improve the quality of life and independence of women aged 65 and above by expanding access to practical support, financial resources, healthcare and trusted care networks.

03 • Why It Matters

Our Purpose

To ensure that growing older does not mean becoming invisible, financially insecure or disconnected from the support systems that make life dignified and fulfilling.

A Note From the Founder

OBIK Origins
“Caring for an older loved one and being able to provide consistent support are not always the same thing.”

I began to understand this personally, through my relationship with my grandmother and her two sisters, the three women I fondly think of as my three grandmothers.

I support them with an allowance that helps meet their basic needs and maintain some financial independence. Over time, I came to appreciate that this support went beyond the money itself. It gave them financial freedom. They could make decisions about their own lives, meet their needs, and maintain a sense of independence.

For some women, the challenge is coordinating support with family members who live elsewhere. For others, it is access to food, financial resources, healthcare or simply having someone who can help them navigate the services available to them.

But during my own groundwork and research into the realities of aging, I encountered a woman whose circumstances stayed with me.

She was an older woman living in extremely difficult conditions. Her home reflected years of hardship, and she appeared physically exhausted. Her sons weren't visiting, and she had no reliable support. At the time, she survived on little more than porridge.

I remember thinking about how someone could reach that stage of life and have so little to fall back on. No dependable income, no one consistently checking in, and seemingly no one to turn to when even the most basic needs became difficult to meet.

That thinking became OBIK.

That encounter stayed with me. It made the issue feel much bigger than the support I was able to give my own family. It made me think about the countless older women whose circumstances are far less secure, and who may be navigating aging without the financial, healthcare, or community support they need.

I began to understand that caring about older people and having the systems to support them are two very different things.

I also began to reflect on the support systems already in place. While government social protection programs provide important assistance to some older persons, many women remain outside their reach, face barriers to accessing available benefits, or find that the support they receive is insufficient to meet their everyday needs. For others, the challenge is not knowing what they are entitled to or having someone to help them navigate the process.

Some women have families living far away. Others experience financial insecurity, limited access to healthcare, or a lack of reliable community support. Often, these challenges overlap.

I came to realize there is space for complementary support, one that works alongside existing public systems, helps women access the assistance available to them, and addresses some of the needs that remain unmet.

But these women are not helpless. They have raised families, built businesses, worked their land, nurtured communities, and contributed to others' lives. Growing older should not mean losing the ability to make choices, access support, or live with a sense of security.

This is why OBIK exists.

We are building practical support systems that help older women access financial assistance, healthcare, and trusted community connections, while enabling families to remain involved in the wellbeing of those they love, wherever they may be.

Our work is built around three interconnected programs: Diaspora Care, Upkeep & Social Protection, and Health Access & Mobility.

We are at the beginning of this journey, developing these programs and building partnerships with women, families, healthcare providers, community organizations, and institutions that share our belief in a better experience of aging.

There is something else I believe we should all remember: we are all aging.

The older woman we pass today represents a future that many of us hope to experience. Growing older isn't guaranteed, and the opportunity to do so is a privilege. But that privilege should not depend on how much money we have, where we live, or whether someone happens to be available to look after us.

We should be building the kind of world we would want to grow old in, one where support is accessible, dignity is not negotiable, and independence is respected at every stage of life.

At OBIK, we believe aging deserves better than chance and goodwill. It deserves intention, reliable support, and collective responsibility.

If you share this belief, I invite you to help build what we are creating through funding, partnerships, expertise, or collaboration.

Dalcy C.

Founder, OBIK
